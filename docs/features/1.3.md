## Contract

- **Objective:** on the first Explore visit after onboarding, show the chosen interests as removable
  filters with "Show everything" beside them. When an interest has nothing in the area, or location is
  off, say so and offer one explicit widening. Never relax a filter for the viewer.
- **Allowed files:** Explore first-arrival state (`src/components/explore/`), the location hook, the
  onboarding location step, existing primitives, targeted tests and browser scripts.
- **Prerequisites verified:** 1.2 committed and green; batch 00 baseline.
- **Out of scope:** camera, selection, filter or scroll restoration (2.2, Later); the social context
  line (1.4, Later); moving the desktop results drawer off the map (batch 05); native.
- **Preflight:** not required (UI only; no schema, permission or payment change).

## Changes

- `src/components/explore/arrivalNotice.ts` (new): the rules as pure functions. An interest is called
  empty only when every result in the area is loaded; otherwise it is unknown and nothing is said.
  Nothing is said while loading or after a failed query. At most one widening, offered once.
- `src/components/explore/FirstArrival.tsx` (new): a raised surface with the message, a "Show
  everything" chip, one "Remove <interest>" chip per filter, a status line, and "Search a wider area".
- `ExploreClient.tsx`: the strip sits **on the map** at every width, and at the top of the list in the
  phone's list mode. Before this, the welcome message lived only inside the list panel, so a newcomer
  on a phone in map mode never saw it. "Search a wider area" doubles the searched span around the same
  centre and keeps every filter. The notice waits until the map has reported its own area.
- `useGeolocation.ts`: a permission already refused is reported as denied without prompting.
  `WelcomeFlow.tsx` notes a refused prompt in `sessionStorage` for the tab (a flag only, never a
  position) so the first map can say it starts in a default area.
- **Found by the required checks and fixed here** (all pre-existing):
  - `MapCanvas.tsx`: with reduced motion on, an instant map jump never reported its new area, so
    discovery kept querying the old one. Bounds are now read again after the move has rendered. Without
    this the widening did nothing for reduced-motion users.
  - Two accessible-name mismatches on the signed-in map (WCAG 2.5.3): the Filters button with an
    active count, and the profile avatar link.
  - A 4–11px horizontal overflow of the search row at 200% text.
- `tests/first-arrival.test.ts` (new); `scripts/e2e-onboarding.mjs` extended; the golden journey in
  `scripts/e2e.mjs` now goes first map → its filtered list → a place → first save, and checks a later
  visit is not a first arrival.

Migrations: none. Dependencies: none. Flags: none added. Demo and Supabase share this client code.

## Choices recorded (reversible; not founder decisions)

- **The one widening is "Search a wider area"**, because it keeps the viewer's interests. "Show
  everything" is always on the strip as the other explicit choice. After the widening is used and
  something is still missing, the strip says so and offers no second widening.
- **Denied location always offers the widening** once results are in, since the starting area is a
  guess.
- **Per-interest emptiness uses loaded results**, so in a dense area with more than one page it stays
  silent rather than guess.

## Adversarial review

- **Silent relaxation.** No code path changes `interests`, `category`, `kind` or the area except a
  tap handler. The widening changes the map area only; the browser test asserts the tags in the wider
  query are identical.
- **False "nothing here".** Guarded against loading, failed queries, incomplete result pages, the
  moment between tapping the widening and the map reporting its new area, and the server's guessed
  first area.
- **Dead tap.** The widening could be tapped before the map had loaded and did nothing; the notice
  now waits for the map.
- **Privacy.** Only "the location prompt was refused" is stored, per tab. No coordinates.
- **Duplicate regions.** Two copies of the strip exist in the page; only one is displayed at a time
  (the other is `display: none`), verified by role queries in the browser.

## Review fixes (2026-11-12)

[Codex's review](batch-01-codex-review.md) found two faults in the first-map strip. Both were
reproduced, fixed and covered by checks that fail without the fix.

**1. Text search offered a widening that could not run.** During a text search the strip treated the
search's results as the area's, so a search with no matches showed "Nothing in this area…" and
"Search a wider area", and the button did nothing.

- Fix: `arrivalNotice()` takes `textSearch` and, when it is true, reports nothing about the area and
  offers no widening (no empty-interest line, no location line, no button). `ExploreClient` sets it
  from the typed query as well as the settled one, so the notice goes quiet at the first keystroke.
  The query and the interest filters are untouched. The search keeps its own empty state ("Nothing
  matches" on the results card, "No matches for “…”" in the list) and its own scope controls
  ("Search this area" / "Search everywhere").
- Leaving the search brings the area notice back from the area's own results, and the widening then
  runs.

**2. At 200% text the results card covered the strip and the navigation covered its lower controls.**

- Fix: the strip, the status chips and the phone's results card now share one column that runs from
  under the top bar to above the bottom navigation (`data-map-overlay` in `ExploreClient`). The card
  sits at the foot; the strip takes what is left and scrolls inside its own box when it needs more.
  Nothing overlaps at any text size.
- Found by the new keyboard check and fixed with it: at 200% the chip row scrolled sideways and Chrome
  left a focused chip mostly clipped, and "Search a wider area" was wider than the strip. In a narrow
  strip (under 17rem: a phone at about 130% text or more) the chips now stack and wrap their labels.
  At normal size the row still scrolls sideways, and a chip that takes focus is brought wholly into
  view (checked with twelve interests).
